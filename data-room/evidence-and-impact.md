# AGIS Evidence and Impact Notes

**Bryan Garcia Rodriguez · Pioneer Innovations Collective LLC · 2026-11-26**

## 1. What the evidence shows, and what it does not

AGIS has no field results yet. This document collects the third-party
evidence that justifies running a pilot, and is explicit about the boundary
between that evidence and an AGIS result.

## 2. Related-system evidence

A greenhouse tomato experiment at the University of Zimbabwe (Munyaradzi et
al., 2020) compared four irrigation treatments: a soil-moisture-controlled
system used up to 74% less water than local grower practice, with no
statistically significant yield difference across treatments. This supports
the mechanism AGIS targets (controlled irrigation reducing water use without
hurting yield) under one crop and one set of conditions. It is not an AGIS
result, not a forecast, and not evidence of increased income.

FAO estimated in 2020 that 1.2 billion people lived in agricultural areas
with very high water constraints. That is a broad exposure figure for
context, not a count of greenhouse growers or potential AGIS users.

## 3. AGIS's own evidence

- IEEE SoutheastCon 2025 publication: a community-driven framework for
  automated greenhouse research and education
  ([doi:10.1109/SoutheastCon56624.2025.10971557](https://doi.org/10.1109/SoutheastCon56624.2025.10971557)).
  The verified abstract describes historical and real-time data and proposes
  analytics, perception, and federated learning. It states no field impact
  estimates for AGIS.
- Firmware: build- and simulation-verified integrated irrigation loop. No
  hardware measurements.
- Demo: modeled scenario outputs only.

## 4. What a pilot would measure

The pilot is designed to produce the evidence AGIS lacks, on a crop-cycle
comparison against current practice:

- **Resource outcomes:** applied water (L), water per marketable kilogram,
  pumping energy.
- **Economic outcomes:** marketable yield, saleable prices, input and repair
  costs, grower net margin with and without unpaid labor valued.
- **Operational outcomes:** uptime, manual overrides, fault and repair
  record, continued use, and reasons for abandonment from growers who stop.

A process evaluation, not an RCT, is the honest design for a first pilot:
it identifies problems and estimates ranges rather than claiming causal
impact. Pilot variability and attrition data would inform a powered
follow-on study if the results warrant one.

## 5. Claim hygiene

Every external document in this data room marks modeled or proposed figures
as such. Where the applications required a gap (operating country, measured
traction, priced BOM, revenue figures), the gap is named rather than filled
with an estimate. This is deliberate: a diligence reviewer who finds one
inflated claim discounts all of them.
