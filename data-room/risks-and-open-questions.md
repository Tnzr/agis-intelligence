# AGIS Risks and Open Questions

**Bryan Garcia Rodriguez · Pioneer Innovations Collective LLC · 2026-11-26**

What a diligence reviewer will ask, answered honestly.

## Technical

- **The firmware has not run on hardware.** It compiles and the control logic
  passes simulation tests, but sensor noise, PWM behavior on a real pump,
  watchdog handling, and power-loss recovery are unmeasured. Mitigation:
  bench validation is step 1 of the roadmap, before any field claim.
- **The PCB has not been fabricated or tested.** GreenPrints designs are
  published as reviewed images only.
- **No priced BOM.** Historical parts totals ($173.87 minimal, $404.85
  complete) are US parts lists, not quotes. Pilot-country landed cost,
  assembly, and install labor are unknown until step 3 of the roadmap.
- **Sole founder, one engineer.** All firmware, hardware, and demo work is
  one person. Delivery risk is real; the pilot plan leans on a partner
  organization's local technician capacity for installation and maintenance.

## Market and adoption

- **No evidence of demand yet.** No grower interviews, willingness-to-pay
  data, or letters of intent. The WFP/DIV drafts list these as gaps.
- **Substitutes are cheap.** Manual watering and $20 timers are the actual
  competition; AGIS must beat them on metered water savings and operator
  time, not on features.
- **Grant dependence.** If WFP/DIV/SBIR all pass, the pilot still proceeds
  slower via crowdfunding or a paid nursery pilot, but 2026-2027 timelines
  assume at least one grant lands.

## Program

- **No pilot partner.** The gating dependency for both UN applications and
  the pilot itself. Shortlist is ready; outreach is the next action.
- **WFP form requires current WFP-country operations.** A signed
  implementing-partner arrangement is the qualifying path; a US-only entity
  does not qualify on its own.
- **DIV requires prior real-world use.** A field test must exist before
  Stage 1 submission; the applications are held for this reason.

## Legal and operational

- **Sole founder, Florida LLC.** Corporate structure is simple and clean for
  US grants; a local partner entity is required for UNICEF Venture Fund and
  practical for in-country work.
- **Open-source strategy vs. defensibility.** Competitors can copy the
  design; the moat is the deployed reference, the partner network, and
  published measurements, not secrecy.

## Questions open to funders/partners

- Which pilot metrics does the funder weight most: water savings, net
  margin, or adoption/continued-use?
- Is a process evaluation acceptable at Stage 1 scale, or is a powered
  comparison expected?
- What partner type is preferred: university (Zamorano, La Molina), public
  institute (AGROSAVIA, INIAP, IDIAF, CARDI), or NGO (Vivamos Mejor, IICA)?
